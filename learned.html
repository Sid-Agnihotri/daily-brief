Quantum computing

1 of 12 stops. Next: Quantum gates: NOT (X) and Hadamard.

  1. Qubits & superposition
    Day 1, Oct 15, 2026. Read it again
    What to remember
    • A qubit's state is a unit-length vector in a 2-D space, written α|0⟩ + β|1⟩.
    • The amplitudes obey |α|² + |β|² = 1. "Superposition" just means a linear combination.
    • Measuring gives 0 or 1 with probabilities |α|² and |β|², and leaves the qubit in that basis state.

Software engineering

1 of 14 stops. Next: HTTP & APIs: methods, status codes, REST.

  1. Anatomy of a web request
    Day 1, Oct 15, 2026. Read it again
    What to remember
    • Before any HTTP is sent, the browser has to find the server (DNS) and open an encrypted connection (TCP + TLS).
    • The server routes the request to code that builds a response; status codes like 200 and 304 tell the browser what happened.
    • Rendering repeats the fetch cycle for every image, script and stylesheet, so most performance work is about skipping steps.

  1. How LLMs work: word vectors & next-word prediction
    Day 1, Oct 15, 2026. Read it again
    What to remember
    • LLMs represent each word as a long vector, and words with similar meanings sit close together.
    • A stack of layers refines those vectors: attention pulls in context from the prompt, feed-forward layers add learned knowledge.
    • Training is next-word prediction over huge amounts of text, with backpropagation adjusting billions of weights.

  1. Saturn grew a ten-sided storm, and no one knows why now
    Day 1, Oct 15, 2026. Read it again
    What to remember
    • Saturn now has a ten-sided wave around its south pole, a counterpart to the long-lived north-pole hexagon.
    • It rides a jet stream, extends through several atmospheric layers, drifts east about 10 km/h, and each side is longer than Earth is wide.
    • It appears to have formed between 2017 and 2023, and why it formed now is unknown.
